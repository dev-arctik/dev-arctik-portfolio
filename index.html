2025 — 2026 · lead developer

Mediator Gabby

AI-assisted mediation for separating parents, live for Better Parenting Plan. A mediator registers the couple; each parent then works through their agreements alone with the agent in a private breakout room, and the system compares both sets of answers into an agree / partly / disagree map the mediator carries into the real session.

Thirteen topics across two independently scoped rooms, custody and financial, each its own LangGraph with its own checkpointed state so a couple can be scoped to either or both. Parents never self-register — the mediator is the only way in. FastAPI and MongoDB behind it, React in front, Stripe subscriptions and trials around it.

The first version ran the joint session too. Professional mediators said that overreached, so I rebuilt it around them: the agent collects and compares, the human mediates.

2026 · my own product

Hundi

A manual-first personal finance manager for Android, for salaried Indians who would rather not hand an app their bank credentials. Income arrives, you confirm it, and it splits by percentage into typed buckets — investments and savings planned first, expenses take what is left. Nothing is linked and nothing is auto-logged; every rupee is an entry you made on purpose.

Productised from the Notion tracker I ran on myself from 2022 to 2024 — five linked databases, three employers, every salary and category logged by hand. The discipline held, the tooling didn't, so Hundi is that same system with reminders, confirmation prompts and a monthly view. Named for the hundi: both a medieval Indian bill of exchange and a temple collection box, which is where the piggy banks come from.

2026 · local AI

Maya

A private voice agent living on my own Linux home server, named after the assistant in Iron Man. Ask it about the machine and it answers out loud while the terminal fills in the detail. Nothing leaves the hardware — no cloud, no subscription, nobody else holding the transcript.

The interesting part is the budget: 4 GB of VRAM. Ollama holds the GPU while Whisper and Piper run on CPU so they never compete for it. Every tool is read-only by design, so Maya can describe the server but never change it.

2026 · open source

remotion-video-mcp

An MCP server that hands Claude a video editor. Thirteen tools cover scaffolding a Remotion project, writing and reordering scenes, syncing narration to word-level timestamps, capturing preview frames and rendering the MP4. A single composition.json is the source of truth and the React components are generated from it, so you describe the video and nobody touches a timeline.

The journey so far

Mechanical engineering degree, WordPress first job, AI agents now. Nothing here was a straight line — every step started as something I taught myself on the side.

  1. 2025 — now

    Whole products, not features

    Gabby for Better Parenting Plan is the clearest example — I wrote 419 of the 582 commits across a seven-person codebase, then built the follow-up when real mediators told us the AI was doing too much. These days the work runs from the first conversation with a client to the thing that actually runs in front of their users.

  2. throughout

    Freelancing, and the half that isn't code

    Freelance work put me in front of people who know their business inside out and know nothing about software. Learning to sit in that conversation and come back with something buildable turned out to matter as much as anything technical — it's the reason the Gabby rebuild happened at all, because I could hear what the mediators were really asking for underneath the feature request.

  3. Sept 2024

    LangChain in September, the job title in November

    Same move as Flutter, second time around. I started pushing agent experiments to my own repos in September, LangGraph a month later, and by November it was my actual title at Gradscaler. Learning in the open, slightly ahead of the job, is the only career strategy that has worked for me twice.

  4. 2023 — 2024

    Backends and the messy middle

    Mobile developer first, Flutter and Kotlin. Then at Alendei I moved off the web team into support and built a customer lifecycle system on Zoho webhooks and the WhatsApp API. First time I owned the whole path — request in, data out, and a real person on the other end waiting on a reply.

  5. 2022

    Learned Flutter on my own time

    Still writing WordPress for a living, I spent evenings drawing Sierpinski triangles and Barnsley ferns with Flutter's CustomPainter, purely to learn it. The commits are still public. Seven months later that was the job.

  6. 2020 — 2022

    Websites, alongside a mechanical engineering degree

    I was studying mechanical engineering and building sites on the side — WordPress and Figma for ArctikCircle, plus whatever I could teach myself at night. The handle on this site is left over from that first team.
